# Report 1 — Niche Selection and Unit Economics

**Decision context:** Ottawa/Ontario local lead generation, applied to an OAS backflow-service pilot  
**Research date:** 2026-07-15  
**Evidence rule:** Episode 315 claims are hypotheses, not proof. “Fact” below means a claim supported by a current primary source; “inference” is a reasoned implication; “unknown” requires field data.

## Executive conclusion

Backflow testing is a credible niche for a tightly scoped OAS pilot, but the evidence supports a **real Ottawa service business with an owned lead asset**, not the podcast’s pure “rank a shell site and rent the calls” model.

The demand foundation is unusually strong: Ottawa’s current Water By-law requires approved devices to be tested on installation and annually thereafter; the City also describes five-year premise-isolation surveys and assigns compliance responsibilities to property owners. The affected market includes severe- and moderate-hazard industrial, commercial, institutional, and multi-residential properties. Testing and repair must be performed by a City-defined Qualified Person. These are facts from City sources.

What is not established is whether enough of that regulated demand begins with a Google search, what an Ottawa customer pays, how many devices are bundled into a visit, the repair attach rate, the number and strength of competitors, or OAS’s fully loaded contribution margin. Search volume is especially uncertain because a property owner may respond to a City/BSI notice, reuse an incumbent plumber, or obtain a tester from a compliance system rather than search the open web.

The recommendation is therefore **conditional go**: run a demand-and-economics validation before treating SEO as an asset investment. OAS should use its real identity, disclose who performs the work, validate credentials and insurance, and initially serve only the City of Ottawa. A pure lead-generation company is ineligible for a Google Business Profile under Google’s current rules; an actual service business that meets customers in person may be eligible. That difference materially changes the model.

## Episode hypothesis

Episode 315 advances this operating hypothesis:

1. Search for a high-value local service in a smaller city.
2. Treat weak organic pages and low-review map results as evidence of low competition.
3. Build a geo-service website, often on an exact-match domain.
4. Rank it, route calls to a provider, and charge fixed rent; use revenue share later.
5. Prefer niches where the owner is reachable and a closed job can support a meaningful lead fee.

The useful parts are the emphasis on transaction value, buyer availability, provider responsiveness, and measuring calls. The weak parts are the jump from “weak SERP” to “profitable market,” the absence of a defensible demand model, and the assumption that historical U.S. rank-and-rent outcomes transfer to Ottawa in 2026.

## Current evidence

### 1. There is recurring compliance demand, but its size is unknown

**Fact — high confidence.** Ottawa’s Backflow Prevention Program monitors annual testing. Water By-law No. 2025-227 states that an approved backflow device is tested on installation and annually thereafter by a Qualified Person. The City’s standards page also describes a five-year premise-isolation survey. Property owners are responsible for surveys, installation, testing, and follow-up. City guidance says impacted occupancies include industrial, commercial, institutional, and multi-residential uses classified as severe or moderate risk.

**Inference — medium-high confidence.** Recurrence makes this more attractive than an episodic emergency service: a completed test can create a renewal list, while a failed test can lead to repair work. The economic asset may therefore be the permissioned compliance/reminder relationship, not merely the ranking.

**Unknown.** No current, public City source found in this research gives the total number of affected private properties, registered devices, annual tests, failed tests, or open compliance cases. The City points registered owners with a CCN number to BSI Online for device compliance and qualified testers. That is useful operationally but does not establish the public addressable market.

### 2. Qualification constrains supply and supports trust

**Fact — high confidence.** The City says a person testing and repairing a device must be an OWWA Certified Cross Connection Control Specialist, with additional trade requirements set out for work such as installation, relocation, or replacement. OWWA maintains a directory of currently certified testers. Ontario treats plumber as a compulsory trade and Skilled Trades Ontario provides a public register.

**Inference — medium confidence.** A legitimate OAS operator with current OWWA and trade credentials has a stronger trust position than an anonymous lead site. Credential scarcity may support value, but the OWWA directory also reveals competing supply. Count Ottawa-area active testers and distinguish employees from businesses before assuming scarcity.

### 3. Search tools provide estimates, not a market answer

**Fact — high confidence.** Google Keyword Planner provides location-specific average monthly searches, advertiser competition, bid ranges, and forecasts. Google states that volumes are rounded, default to a 12-month average, fluctuate, and forecasts are less accurate in small geographies. Advertiser “competition” is not organic SEO difficulty.

**Fact — high confidence.** Google Trends uses sampled, normalized data scaled from 0 to 100. Low-volume terms may display zero, and apparent low-volume spikes can be statistical noise. It is a comparison tool, not an absolute demand count.

**Inference — high confidence.** For Ottawa backflow, use Keyword Planner for directional demand and Trends for terminology/seasonality, then validate with paid-search or live conversion data. A zero in Trends cannot kill the niche; a non-zero value cannot prove unit economics. Manual SERP review should record result type, actual local provider, paid placements, City pages, directories, and query intent—not just review count.

### 4. The map shortcut in the episode is not available to a lead shell

**Fact — high confidence.** Google’s Business Profile eligibility policy explicitly lists lead-generation agents or companies as ineligible. An eligible business generally must make in-person contact with customers during stated hours. Only owners or authorized representatives may verify/manage a profile; Google says the authoritative phone and website must be verifiable by the business owner.

**Inference — high confidence.** OAS should either fulfill the service as the real merchant or transparently market for a real provider that owns its Business Profile. A fabricated geo-service profile would create suspension risk and should not be included in the economic forecast.

## Unit-economics model

Do not price from “calls.” Build the model from completed work.

| Layer | Required input | How to obtain it |
|---|---|---|
| Addressable demand | affected properties/devices, test cadence, surveys due | City/BSI owner data where available; City information request; property-manager interviews |
| Searchable demand | Ottawa impressions by query and month | Keyword Planner; later Search Console; small paid-search validation |
| Capture | organic/ad click share, landing-page conversion | pilot data, separated by mobile/desktop and query |
| Lead quality | valid property, in service area, correct service, contactable | call/form disposition log |
| Sale | booking, show, completion, failure/repair attach | job-management or CRM records |
| Revenue | test price, multi-device fee, survey/repair revenue | actual quotes and invoices, not competitor list prices alone |
| Variable cost | labour, travel, calibration, reporting, merchant fees, materials | time-and-motion observation and bookkeeping |
| Acquisition cost | ads, content, links/promotion, call tracking, sales time | cash ledger plus operator hours |
| Retention | annual renewal rate, property churn, device additions/losses | cohort tracking by property and device |

Core formulas:

- `qualified leads = local impressions × click rate × contact rate × qualification rate`
- `completed jobs = qualified leads × booking rate × completion rate`
- `contribution per job = collected revenue − direct labour − travel − materials − reporting/payment cost`
- `allowable acquisition cost = contribution per first job + probability-weighted future contribution − required profit/risk reserve`
- `SEO payback months = cumulative build/promotion cost ÷ monthly incremental contribution`

Model three cases—downside, base, upside—and change one uncertain variable at a time. Do not count repair revenue until a real cohort establishes failure and conversion rates. Do not count annual renewals until customers have given reminder consent and a renewal process exists.

## Ottawa-specific application

Start with one offer and one jurisdiction:

**Offer:** annual backflow test and submission support for City of Ottawa ICI and affected multi-residential properties. Keep five-year surveys, failed-device repair, installation and permit coordination as separately qualified extensions. Do not imply OAS can perform any task until the applicable City definition, trade credential, insurance, equipment calibration, and reporting workflow are verified.

**Best initial buyers:** small property-management firms, owner-managed commercial buildings, facilities coordinators, and multi-site local operators. They are more likely than a homeowner to understand the compliance trigger and may hold multiple devices. Large public and institutional buyers often use procurement or standing offers; treat those as a distinct sales channel, not ordinary web leads.

**Geography:** City of Ottawa only. Do not mix Gatineau into one economics model: Quebec licensing, language, municipal rules, and search behaviour require separate research.

**Channel hypothesis:** use search as one channel, but test direct outreach to property managers, facility associations, existing plumbing relationships, and annual reminders. If direct/renewal demand dominates, the correct business may be a compliance-service book supported by SEO—not a rank-and-rent portfolio.

## Unknowns and conflicts

- **Unknown:** exact Ottawa monthly search volume for “backflow testing,” French variants, device-specific terms, “annual test,” “BSI,” and problem-led queries.
- **Unknown:** affected property/device count and percentage already tied to incumbent contractors.
- **Unknown:** current market prices, multi-device discounting, payment terms, and fully loaded margin.
- **Unknown:** annual failure rate, average repair value, and permissioned renewal rate.
- **Unknown:** organic click opportunity after ads, Maps, City pages, directories, and AI features.
- **Conflict:** the episode treats exact-match naming as a meaningful shortcut; current Google documentation says keywords in the domain alone have hardly any ranking effect and an exact-match-domain system limits excess credit.
- **Conflict:** the episode’s low-review map heuristic assumes a lead site can enter Maps; Google currently excludes lead-generation companies.
- **Conflict:** regulated recurring demand does not necessarily equal open-market search demand.

## Practical pilot exercise

Complete this before a major content or link investment:

1. **Credential gate:** verify current OWWA standing, Skilled Trades Ontario status, insurance, calibrated test equipment, City/BSI submission access, and the precise services OAS may perform.
2. **Demand inventory:** export 24 months where available from Keyword Planner for at least 30 English and French/service/problem variants, targeted to Ottawa. Save date, geography, language, monthly pattern, bids and the tool’s caveats.
3. **SERP sample:** manually inspect the top 20 commercial-intent queries from an Ottawa location on mobile and desktop. Record ads, local pack, organic providers, directories, government results, landing-page quality and actual service identity.
4. **Supply count:** use OWWA’s tester list, STO verification, City/BSI information available to owners, and provider websites to estimate active Ottawa businesses—not merely individuals.
5. **Ten buyer interviews:** ask how testing is triggered, how a provider is chosen, typical device count, incumbent satisfaction, procurement friction, missed-deadline consequences, repair handling and acceptable response time. Do not sell during the evidence portion.
6. **Quote and job-cost sample:** obtain or produce five real, comparable quotes and time at least three legitimate jobs. Separate test, travel, reporting, repair and follow-up labour.
7. **Controlled acquisition test:** publish one transparent OAS landing page and, if approved, run a small exact/phrase paid-search experiment with a preset loss limit. Track qualified, booked, completed and collected—not only calls.
8. **Decision memo:** calculate downside/base/upside economics and state whether the bottleneck is demand, ranking, conversion, capacity or buyer trust.

## Completion test

This research is complete only when OAS can answer, with dated evidence:

- Who is legally/operationally eligible to perform each offered service?
- How many relevant searches and non-search compliance opportunities exist by month?
- What percentage of inquiries are qualified, booked, completed and collected?
- What is contribution per completed test, per property and per annual cohort?
- What is the maximum affordable acquisition cost and downside payback period?
- Which three competitors win each major query, and why would a buyer choose OAS?
- What is the go/repair/stop threshold if organic ranking or a Business Profile never materializes?

Passing means all inputs are measured or explicitly bounded and the downside case does not require unverified repair, renewal, map-ranking or revenue-share assumptions to work.

## Failure modes

- Mistaking a compliance obligation for Google search volume
- Treating advertiser competition or review count as organic difficulty
- Building before confirming a real buyer/provider and service capacity
- Counting every call as a qualified lead or every quote as revenue
- Ignoring travel, reporting, calibration, insurance and sales labour
- Depending on a Business Profile that the operating entity is not eligible to own
- Pricing from competitor websites without matching scope/device count
- Mixing City of Ottawa and Quebec rules in one offer
- Assuming renewals without consent, reminders and cohort evidence
- Scaling pages or locations before one pilot produces collected contribution
