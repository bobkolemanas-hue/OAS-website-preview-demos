# Report 6 — Pricing, Contracts and Revenue Share

**Decision context:** Ottawa/Ontario local lead-generation business  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-07-15  
**Evidence convention:** **Fact** = cited authority; **vendor benchmark** = directional vendor data; **inference** = proposed OAS rule; **unknown** = pilot or professional review required. Not legal or tax advice.

## Executive conclusion

Do not begin with the episode’s 20% revenue share, long fixed “site rent,” or equity-like arrangement. They require stable volume, trusted accounting, agreed attribution and proven economics.

Use a staged commercial model:

1. **Thirty-day capped PQL pilot:** predefine validity, allow 48-hour evidence-based disputes, charge accepted leads, and cap spend and volume.
2. **After 20 leads and 60 days:** offer a reliable partner fixed minimum plus PQL overage, or fixed rent based on trailing economics; review quarterly.
3. **After six months of reconciled lead-to-cash data:** consider collected-revenue share for a jointly managed program. Avoid net-profit shares and pilot-stage equity.

The contract is the measurement system: define acceptance, attribution, cash basis, refunds, taxes, audit evidence, data, asset ownership, service standards, exclusivity, termination and non-circumvention. Ontario counsel, a CPA and OAS’s insurer should review it before launch.

## Episode hypothesis to test

The captions present the following as the guest’s experience, not verified market evidence:

- Start with referral/commission, then convert to fixed rent once closes are visible; allegedly most sites use fixed fees, while larger relationships use revenue share.
- The guest describes 20% of gross in some niches and a higher percentage of “net” in material-heavy work, supported by CRM/payment visibility.
- Autopay and asset ownership preserve operator control; later arrangements may include “profit interest,” phantom equity or equity.

The episode supplies no contracts, audited revenue, refund/tax treatment, acquisition cost, failed-site denominator or Ontario compliance evidence.

## Model comparison

| Model | What triggers payment | Strengths | Main risks | OAS use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed monthly rent | Access to an agreed lead stream for a month | Predictable; low reconciliation burden; contractor keeps upside | Contractor bears volume/quality volatility; repricing conflict; asset suspension risk | Use only after stable trailing data |
| Pay per qualified lead | Contractor accepts a lead meeting written criteria | Low entry risk; fast learning; transparent unit economics | Validity disputes; rewards volume rather than outcomes | **Recommended first pilot** |
| Referral/success fee | Appointment, completed job, or collected invoice | Stronger outcome alignment | Longer cash cycle; attribution/circumvention disputes; regulated-profession restrictions | Possible second-stage variant |
| Revenue share | Percentage of defined attributable collected revenue | OAS shares upside and can fund more growth | Deep audit/data needs; refunds, materials, overhead and bad-debt disputes; privacy exposure | Only after mature evidence and counsel review |
| Profit share | Percentage of “net profit” | Theoretical alignment | “Net” is manipulable through cost allocation; intrusive accounting; partnership/agency risk | Avoid |
| Profit interest / phantom equity / real equity | Contractual or ownership interest in business value/profits | Potential long-term upside | Corporate, securities, tax, valuation, governance, dilution, exit and minority-rights complexity | Not for the pilot; separate transaction team required |

“Referral fee” is not a universal legal category. Regulated professions may restrict fee splitting: Ontario dentists cannot share payments tied to advertising, and lawyers generally cannot split fees with non-licensees [S12, S13]. Check every niche.

## Auditable unit economics

The contractor must supply evidence for each input; OAS should not use podcast claims or generic close-rate tables.

Let:

- **L** = accepted qualified leads;
- **A** = average pre-HST collected invoice for an attributable job;
- **C** = accepted-lead-to-completed-job rate;
- **P** = share of invoiced job revenue actually collected after refunds/bad debt;
- **M** = contribution margin after agreed direct job costs, before OAS marketing fee and fixed overhead;
- **B** = maximum share of contribution the contractor will spend to acquire the job.

Then:

`expected collected revenue per lead = A × C × P`

`expected contribution per lead = A × C × P × M`

`maximum rational PQL price = A × C × P × M × B`

**Illustration only—not an Ottawa benchmark:** if A=$500, P=95%, M=55% and B=30%, the maximum PQL price changes materially with close rate:

| Close rate C | Expected contribution/lead | Illustrative maximum PQL |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 20% | $52.25 | $15.68 |
| 35% | $91.44 | $27.43 |
| 50% | $130.63 | $39.19 |

Replace every illustrative input with observed Ottawa ticket, margin and close data before pricing.

For a revenue share of **S** on collected revenue, contractor contribution after OAS’s fee is approximately:

`attributable collected revenue × (M − S)`

At 25% contribution margin, a 20% gross share leaves 5% before overhead and profit. Verify the partner’s cost structure before choosing a percentage.

For fixed rent, use:

`trailing median monthly accepted leads × agreed PQL equivalent × reliability adjustment`

The adjustment should reflect volatility and Google/SEO concentration. Use quarterly resets and a suspension clause for material lead loss.

## Contract definitions that remove most disputes

### Qualified lead

Recommended definition: a new prospect requesting an approved service in the agreed territory/hours, with valid contact/property information, who can legally be served and consents to sharing with the selected independent provider. Exclude spam, vendors, job seekers, wrong numbers, out-of-scope/area work, defined-window duplicates and proven existing customers.

Require a timestamped pre-lead CRM record to prove an existing customer. Count a multi-device or property-manager inquiry as one opportunity, not one lead per device.

### Acceptance and disputes

- Give each call/form a unique ID and timestamp.
- Allow 48 hours to claim invalidity with contract reason and evidence; silence after proven delivery means acceptance.
- Decide routine disputes from the ledger; review patterns monthly. Use credits/replacements unless cash refunds are expressly defined.

### Attributable collected revenue

Use a cash-based definition for revenue share:

> Cash actually cleared for eligible work arising from an accepted OAS lead, excluding HST and separately identified refundable deposits, less refunds, chargebacks and written-off bad debt.

Specify treatment of permit/BSI fees, materials, subcontractors, surcharges, financing, recurring work, repairs, change orders, referrals and repeats.

Avoid “net profit.” For contribution-based shares, enumerate permitted variable-cost deductions; exclude unbounded overhead, owner salary, depreciation and head-office allocations.

### Attribution window and evidence

Set service-specific windows, first/last-touch treatment and existing-customer lookback. Match normalized phone/email/address to a unique ID:

`source → lead → contact → appointment → estimate → authorization → invoice → cleared payment/refund`

Show only attributable rows, with 30- or 60-day payment/refund true-ups. Give OAS a limited export or CRM view, not full accounting access. PIPEDA requires meaningful consent, appropriate purposes and limited disclosure [S5–S7].

### Audit rights

Recommended terms for counsel to refine:

- monthly partner certification;
- relevant-record inspection on notice, at most twice yearly absent a discrepancy;
- privacy-preserving samples, retention/access logs and bilateral confidentiality/breach notice;
- underpayment correction/interest, with audit cost shifted only above a written materiality threshold.

## Ownership and platform compliance

OAS may own the organic domain, content, analytics and routing. Define partner use and termination treatment. The contractor owns its estimates, service records, warranties and accepted-job relationships; OAS retains only necessary attribution/audit records.

Do not copy the episode’s GBP structure. Google lists lead generators as ineligible; profiles must represent the real business and use a phone it directly controls [S2, S3]. Therefore:

- the actual contractor should own/control its legitimate GBP;
- OAS may be an authorized manager under a written relationship;
- an OAS lead-gen brand should not manufacture a “false competitor” or virtual location;
- organic referral pages must clearly explain OAS’s role.

The Competition Bureau assesses literal claims and overall impression [S4]. A footer cannot cure a site that implies OAS is the licensed provider when it only routes leads.

## Core agreement schedule

Ontario counsel should draft/review at least these clauses:

1. Legal names, authority, independent-contractor/no-agency status; territory, services, qualifications and capacity.
2. Lead, duplicate, existing-customer, acceptance and credit definitions.
3. Fee, HST, invoicing, payment, refunds, bad debt, attribution, evidence and audit.
4. Consumer-role disclosure, privacy/consent, recording, security and breach duties.
5. Domain, content, number, analytics, GBP, reviews and customer-record ownership.
6. Response, estimating, workmanship, complaints, warranty, licensing, insurance, WSIB, indemnity and liability.
7. Exclusivity limited by service/territory/hours and conditional on volume, capacity, payment and SLA floors.
8. Narrow, time-limited non-circumvention for identifiable OAS leads—not a broad market restraint.
9. Term, cure/suspension, convenience/cause termination, open-lead transition, true-up, confidentiality, Ontario law and dispute escalation.

## Taxes and accounting

**Fact:** CRA says a business generally must register and start charging GST/HST when taxable supplies exceed the $30,000 small-supplier threshold under the applicable quarter tests; Ontario’s HST rate is 13% [S10, S11]. A commission or marketing service is not automatically outside HST. The invoice should state fees before HST and the applicable tax separately.

**Professional review:** classify the payment; confirm place-of-supply, bad-debt adjustments, reporting, deductibility and revenue recognition. U.S. “profits interest” language is not portable: it is a U.S. partnership-tax construct [S14]. Ontario shares, options, phantom equity or partnership arrangements need Canadian corporate, securities and tax advice [S15].

## Ottawa/backflow application

Backflow economics have at least three distinct products:

- routine annual test and filing;
- five-year premise-isolation survey;
- failed-device repair/replacement and associated permits/retest.

Do not blend them into one lead price. A property manager seeking a portfolio quote is one account opportunity even if it later covers many devices. Define whether a test lead’s later repair is included, separately bonused, or outside the fee. Define who pays/collects BSI administration charges and how they appear on the invoice.

Annual testing creates repeat value but does not justify perpetual revenue share. Charge the initial event; renewals belong to the contractor unless a consented OAS reminder service is separately priced.

If OAS performs regulated service, it becomes provider of record and assumes a different insurance, warranty, permit, privacy and liability model. Separate direct and referred work.

## Skills and tools required

- Spreadsheet/unit-economics model with sensitivity analysis by service type.
- CRM with immutable lead IDs, timestamps, consent, partner, disposition, estimate and invoice/payment joins.
- Call/form tracking configured for privacy; call recording only after clear notice, stated purpose and consent [S7].
- Monthly reconciliation and dispute dashboard; access-controlled partner portal.
- Contract redlining competence and an Ontario commercial/privacy lawyer.
- Bookkeeping chart of accounts separating PQL, fixed rent, referral and revenue-share income; HST tracking; CPA review.
- Partner scorecards and capacity-based routing.
- Partner-recruitment compliance: B2B calls remain subject to Telemarketing Rules [S8]; commercial email/text needs consent or supportable implied consent, identification and unsubscribe [S9].

## Practical pilot exercise

1. Obtain de-identified economics for 20 comparable jobs: pre-HST invoice, direct costs, source, outcome, collection/refund and time-to-close.
2. Test lead/existing-customer definitions against 10 synthetic examples until classification matches.
3. Price a 10-lead/30-day capped PQL pilot using the formula above and conservative inputs.
4. Route real leads with unique IDs. Reconcile weekly; resolve invalidity within 48 hours.
5. At 20 cumulative leads or 60 days, calculate actual C, P, M, dispute rate and contribution after fee. Offer fixed-plus-overage only if both parties remain positive.
6. Do not offer revenue share until six months of statements reconcile to cash and the privacy/audit workflow works without broad accounting-system access.

### Completion test

Completion requires lawyer-approved agreement/disclosure, CPA-confirmed tax treatment, insurer confirmation, 20 reconciled leads with 100% dispositions, disputes below 10%, a reproducible fee, positive downside-case contribution, and termination without losing records, domain control, open-job accountability or earned amounts.

## Unknowns and conflicts

- Actual Ottawa lead volume, close rate, tickets, direct costs, collection lag, refunds and lifetime value by backflow service.
- Whether contractors prefer PQL, fixed rent or success fee and what minimum volume makes administrative work worthwhile.
- Proper insurance classification and limits for OAS as marketer/referrer versus provider.
- Counsel’s view on exclusivity/non-circumvention and niche-specific fee-sharing.
- Customer/renewal ownership for annual compliance work.
- Vendor data location and required contract, notice and safeguards.
- Episode conflict: “20% of gross” may be profitable in high-margin work but destructive in material-heavy work; no universal rate exists.

## Failure modes

- Setting price from the guest’s reported revenue rather than partner contribution economics.
- Undefined qualification/duplicates or invoiced-revenue share without refund/bad-debt true-up.
- “Net profit” without enumerated deductions; full accounting access where a limited export suffices.
- Granting exclusivity without answer/capacity/performance floors.
- Misallocated domain/number/GBP control or concealed/fabricated referral identity.
- Undefined perpetual renewal fees or U.S. “profit interest” language used as an Ontario shortcut.
- No post-termination rules for open leads, warranties, refunds and unpaid fees.
