# Report 3 — Google Business Profile Compliance for an Ottawa Local Lead-Generation Business

**Research date:** 2026-07-15  
**Decision scope:** Whether and how OAS can use Google Business Profile (GBP) while testing an Ottawa/Ontario local lead-generation model.  
**Evidence labels:** **Fact** = directly stated by an authoritative source; **Inference** = reasoned application of that evidence; **Unknown** = requires confirmation from Google, counsel, or the operating facts.

## Executive conclusion

The episode's tactic of creating keyword-and-city profiles that look like independent local service companies is not a compliant starting model for OAS. Google expressly says lead-generation agents or companies are ineligible for Business Profiles. It also requires a profile to represent a real business that has in-person customer contact, use the real-world business name, and use an actual staffed location or a legitimate service-area base. Virtual offices, remote mailboxes, duplicate profiles, and location profiles without separate staff are disallowed.

OAS has two defensible paths:

1. **Operator model:** OAS is the actual backflow service provider, performs in-person work, owns one genuine service-area profile, and routes inquiries to its own operation. This is likely eligible if the real-world identity and operating facts support it.
2. **Authorized-partner model:** A real plumbing/backflow contractor owns its profile and remains primary owner. OAS is added as manager with express consent and provides marketing, tracking, and workflow services. The profile, authoritative phone, website, service area, and claims represent that contractor—not a lead-gen shell.

A third path is a **profile-free organic referral or directory site**. It may publish useful Ottawa backflow information and refer visitors transparently, but it must not impersonate a contractor or rely on thin city clones. Google's Search spam policies identify region-targeted funnels as doorway abuse and mass low-value pages as scaled-content abuse.

**Recommendation:** Pilot OAS as the real Ottawa backflow operator if Alex is actually delivering the work. Do not create a separate keyword-stuffed “Ottawa Backflow Testing” profile merely to capture leads, do not manufacture locations, and do not let OAS hold a partner's profile hostage.

## Episode hypothesis

Episode 315 advances several propositions:

- A city-plus-service business name can enter the map results quickly.
- The site owner can create what the guest calls an apparent or “false” competitor and later have a contractor help verify the profile.
- Multiple DBAs or legal entities can support many local profiles.
- The lead generator can retain control of the asset and replace the receiving contractor.

These are **hypotheses, not evidence of eligibility**. A legal registration or DBA does not override Google's separate requirements that the profile represent an eligible, real-world, customer-facing business at a legitimate location. The episode also predates or does not test today's enforcement environment, provides no policy review, and itself mentions profile suspensions.

## Authoritative current evidence

### 1. Eligibility: real in-person business, not a lead generator

**Fact — high confidence.** Google's [Business eligibility and ownership guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) say an eligible business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours. The same page explicitly lists “lead generation agents or companies” and online-only businesses as ineligible.

**Ottawa inference — high confidence.** A site that only captures backflow inquiries and sells or routes them to an unrelated contractor should not create its own GBP. OAS can qualify when it is the party that attends the property and provides the represented backflow service. A partner contractor can qualify for its own operation.

**Unknown.** Google does not publish a simple percentage-of-work test for a mixed business. If OAS both delivers some work and refers overflow, the profile should still represent the genuine OAS operation, and the referral practice should be transparent. Borderline facts may require Google support or legal review.

### 2. Representation: name, category, address, phone, and website must be genuine

**Fact — high confidence.** Google's [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) require the profile name to reflect the name used consistently in the real world—on the storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers. Adding service or location keywords that are not part of that real-world name can cause suspension. Categories must describe what the business *is*, not act as a keyword list. The address must be the actual operating location. The phone must connect to the individual business location, and the website must represent that location.

**Ottawa inference — high confidence.** Registering a trade name solely to obtain “Ottawa + service” relevance is not enough if invoices, customer communications, vehicles, website, insurance, and other real-world evidence show a different identity. If the real business is Ottawa Agent Systems, use the name customers actually encounter. If OAS later adopts a genuine backflow trade name, it should first make that identity real and consistent; registration alone is not a safe shortcut.

### 3. Service-area businesses, homes, virtual offices, and multiple locations

**Fact — high confidence.** Google allows a service-area business that travels to customers and has no customer-facing storefront to have **one profile for the whole area it serves**. It should hide a residential address. Google permits up to 20 named service areas and says the overall area generally should not extend more than about two hours' driving time from the base. See [Manage your service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en).

**Fact — high confidence.** A rented mailing address or virtual office is ineligible. A co-working office is eligible only when it has clear permanent signage, receives customers during business hours, and is staffed during those hours by the business's own staff. Google prohibits more than one profile per business location. Separate service-area profiles require genuinely separate locations and separate staff.

**Ottawa inference — high confidence.** One OAS technician operating from one Ottawa base should use one hidden-address service-area profile—not separate profiles for Ottawa, Orléans, Kanata, Nepean, Gatineau, or nearby towns. City landing pages can exist only where each page provides distinct, useful service information; they do not create entitlement to another GBP.

**Unknown.** Whether OAS should include Gatineau depends on actual service capability, Quebec licensing/tax/language obligations, and the two-hour guidance—not marketing ambition.

### 4. Ownership and control

**Fact — high confidence.** Only owners or authorized representatives may verify and manage a profile. Google's authorized-representative rules require express owner consent, direct owner involvement in verification, owner awareness of all actions, and prompt transfer on request. They also say the profile phone and website must be the single authoritative assets for the business and verifiable by the owner; website content must be owned and managed by the business owner.

Google's [third-party policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7353941?hl=en) go further: the client must retain ownership or co-ownership at all times; third parties must relinquish access within seven business days after termination; written consent is required to claim a profile; and guarantees of top placement are prohibited. Google's [owner and manager roles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en) let the business remain primary owner while an agency works as manager without password sharing.

**Ottawa inference — high confidence.** The episode's “I want all the control” posture is incompatible with managing a partner's GBP. OAS may own its landing-page software, analytics process, and separately licensed marketing assets, but the operating contractor should own its GBP, business identity, reviews, authoritative website, and phone continuity. Contracts should grant OAS manager access—not primary ownership—and specify an orderly offboarding process.

### 5. Suspension and appeal

**Fact — high confidence.** Google may suspend a profile or restrict the managing account for guideline violations. Its [suspension guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en) says to correct compliance first, then use the appeals tool. Useful evidence includes registration, licence, tax documents, and utility bills whose name and address match the profile. Evidence must be submitted within 60 minutes after opening the evidence form. Do not create a replacement profile while the appeal is pending. Google says not to submit multiple appeals for the same issue; an additional review may be available only after denial.

**Ottawa inference — high confidence.** OAS should maintain a compliance binder before launch: registration, insurance, licences/certificates, invoices, branded materials, address evidence, service photographs, website screenshots, and the owner/manager authorization record. A suspension should trigger a service-continuity plan through the canonical website and phone—not creation of another profile.

### 6. Organic search is not a policy loophole

**Fact — high confidence.** Google's [Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) define doorway abuse to include multiple websites or regional pages made to rank for similar queries and funnel visitors to another destination. They define scaled-content abuse as many pages generated primarily to manipulate rankings without adding value, regardless of whether AI created them. Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) emphasizes original information, real experience, completeness, and value beyond rewritten sources.

**Ottawa inference — medium-high confidence.** A useful OAS page can explain Ottawa's annual-testing process, tester qualifications, what information a property manager should prepare, service scope, and what happens after a failed test. Twenty near-identical suburb pages with swapped place names are risky and strategically weak. Organic-only does not mean consequence-free.

## Compliant model options

### Option A — OAS-owned operating profile (recommended pilot)

- OAS/Alex actually performs the represented backflow work in person.
- One service-area profile, with the home/base address hidden if customers are not received there.
- Real-world business name and specific truthful category.
- Canonical OAS phone and website; no imitation local company.
- Accurate Ottawa-area service boundaries and hours when calls can be handled.
- Reviews requested only from actual OAS customers.

### Option B — Partner-owned profile, OAS as manager

- Contractor is primary owner and completes verification.
- Written authorization; OAS receives manager access.
- Contractor owns or can control the website, number, profile data, and customer history.
- OAS reports GBP performance separately from other channels, as Google's third-party policy requires.
- On termination, OAS removes its access promptly and does not disrupt the contractor's lead flow.

### Option C — Profile-free referral publisher

- Site clearly identifies who operates it and whether requests may be sent to a service partner.
- No GBP for the publisher if it is merely a lead generator.
- Original Ottawa-specific content and transparent contact/routing language.
- No fake addresses, fake reviews, fake staff, fake licences, or cloned city sites.
- Partner profiles remain entirely partner-owned.

## Ottawa application

The City of Ottawa's [Backflow Prevention Program](https://ottawa.ca/en/living-ottawa/drinking-water-stormwater-and-wastewater/drinking-water/drinking-water-programs/backflow-prevention-program/overview) states that annual testing is monitored. The City's [contractor/qualified-person guidance](https://ottawa.ca/en/living-ottawa/drinking-water-stormwater-and-wastewater/drinking-water/drinking-water-programs/backflow-prevention-program/contractors-qualified-persons) points property owners to OWWA/BSI rather than endorsing a City list. Ottawa's [Water By-law 2025-227](https://ottawa.ca/en/living-ottawa/laws-licences-and-permits/laws/laws-z/water-law-no-2025-227) requires registered devices to be tested on installation and annually by a qualified person. OWWA advises checking the tester's current certificate and its [online tester list](https://owwa.ca/certification-training/).

That creates a real service opportunity, but not permission to imply City endorsement. OAS copy should state verifiable credentials, explain the process, and link to official information. It should not claim “City approved,” “official Ottawa tester,” or guaranteed Google placement.

## Unknowns and conflicts to resolve

- **Unknown:** The exact GBP category currently available for OAS's service. Check inside the live profile interface before launch.
- **Unknown:** Whether the OAS corporate/trade name, insurance, invoices, and public identity are sufficiently consistent for verification.
- **Unknown:** Whether Alex will personally fulfill all pilot leads or use a subcontractor; this changes representation and disclosure.
- **Unknown:** Whether a residential base can produce every verification artifact Google requests. The address can be hidden publicly but still must be genuine.
- **Conflict:** A lead-generation owner wants portability between contractors; Google requires the represented business and its owner to control the profile. Portability must live in a profile-free marketing asset, not by disguising profile ownership.
- **Conflict:** Exact-match naming may help discoverability, but adding location/service terms not used in the real world violates Google's name rule.
- **Unknown:** Google may change verification methods or request video evidence without advance notice.

## Pilot exercise

Run a **policy-first GBP readiness drill** before publishing:

1. Choose Option A or B and write a one-sentence statement of who performs the service.
2. Assemble the compliance binder and check that name, address, phone, website, licences, insurance, invoices, and signage/branding agree.
3. Map one genuine Ottawa service area of no more than 20 named areas and within actual operating capacity.
4. Draft the profile exactly as it will appear. Highlight every word that is a location, service, credential, or performance claim and attach evidence.
5. If Option B, have the contractor create/claim the profile and add OAS as manager. Test offboarding on paper.
6. Publish one useful Ottawa backflow page and inspect it against Google's doorway/scaled-content definitions.
7. Document a suspension continuity and appeal checklist before going live.

## Completion test

This knowledge gap is closed for a pilot only when all answers are **yes**:

- Can OAS identify the real in-person service provider without ambiguity?
- Is the profile owner that operating business, with OAS only in its proper role?
- Does every profile field match defensible real-world evidence?
- Is there only one profile per genuine location/staff unit?
- Are virtual offices, mailboxes, keyword stuffing, fake reviews, and lead-gen profiles excluded?
- Can the business continue receiving enquiries if GBP is suspended?
- Can OAS execute an appeal without inventing or altering evidence?
- Has the website passed a people-first/doorway-content review?

## Failure modes

- Creating a profile before there is an actual in-person service operation
- Treating a DBA, mailbox, or server location as proof of a local operating location
- Creating one profile per suburb without separate premises and staff
- Using a partner's licence, address, photos, or reviews as if they belonged to OAS
- Keeping primary ownership of a client's profile or withholding access after termination
- Swapping the receiving contractor while leaving the represented business identity unchanged
- Using CallRail or another number that the operating owner cannot verify or retain
- Publishing AI-generated city/service clones primarily to rank
- Creating a second profile during an appeal
- Promising rankings or implying Google/City endorsement
