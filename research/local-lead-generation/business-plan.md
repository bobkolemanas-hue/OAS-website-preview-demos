# Business plan — OAS Ottawa local demand asset pilot

**Decision date:** 2026-07-15  
**Decision owner:** Alex / Ottawa Agent Systems  
**Evidence base:** Episode 315 caption-derived business model plus eight current research reports  
**Scope:** One Ottawa backflow-service pilot; no assumption that the model generalizes to other niches

## Decision

**Proceed with conditions.** OAS should test an **operator-led, transparent local demand asset**, not copy the episode's classic rank-and-rent implementation.

The model worth testing is:

1. OAS owns the organic website, code, measurement design and accumulated market knowledge.
2. A real, qualified provider is clearly identified and performs the represented work.
3. The customer knows who receives their information and who will quote, contract, invoice and warrant the service.
4. A Google Business Profile exists only for the genuine in-person operator, under that operator's ownership/control.
5. Every inquiry is followed through to qualification, booking, completion, cleared payment and contribution margin.
6. Partner monetization starts with a small pay-per-qualified-lead test, then moves to a fixed fee only after observed economics. Revenue share is a later option, not the opening offer.

Because Alex has commercial-plumbing and backflow experience, the strongest first version is **OAS as the real service operator for the first 20–30 qualified leads**, provided the exact Ottawa qualifications, insurance, equipment, reporting and capacity gates are current. This produces better learning than trying to judge a contractor from call counts. If Alex does not want to fulfill the work, launch with one clearly named exclusive provider plus a qualified backup; the provider owns the service relationship and eligible GBP.

The following episode tactics should not be copied:

- an apparent or “false” local competitor;
- a lead-generation company creating its own GBP;
- keyword-stuffed names, virtual offices or one profile per suburb;
- near-duplicate city/service sites or AI-scaled pages;
- economics based on raw call volume;
- immediate 20% gross-revenue share;
- unrestricted access to a partner's accounting/customer data; or
- profit interests, phantom equity or acquisitions before the lead model is proven.

## Why this is the right interpretation

The episode describes a real business mechanism: own demand-producing assets, route leads to replaceable providers and monetize the flow. Its reported scale—232 sites, roughly $192K–$192.5K monthly revenue, over 90% net margins and two to three owner hours per week—is self-reported, caption-ambiguous in places and not accompanied by portfolio records. It also reflects assets built from 2016 onward, not the cost or success rate of a new 2026 cohort.

Current evidence changes three foundations:

1. **Maps cannot be treated as an owned lead asset.** Google explicitly lists lead-generation agents/companies as ineligible for Business Profiles. The profile and reviews belong with the genuine operating business.
2. **The content shortcut is weaker.** Google says exact-match domains and raw word count are not ranking foundations, and its doorway, scaled-content and link-spam policies directly reach common rank-and-rent tactics.
3. **The hidden bottleneck is the operator/economics chain.** Ottawa has recurring annual backflow requirements, but no evidence yet establishes how many affected owners search Google, what they pay, how much margin remains, how many are locked to incumbents or whether qualified providers will buy/report leads.

That leaves a defensible version: build a real, useful organic asset; use the backflow niche because Alex can judge it; validate demand with interviews and a capped paid-search test; and keep all public identity, consent and partner roles accurate.

## Recommended pilot

### Offer and customer

**Initial offer:** annual backflow device testing and reporting support for eligible City of Ottawa industrial, commercial, institutional and multi-residential properties. Treat premise-isolation surveys, failed-device repair/replacement, installation and permits as separate service lanes that appear only after current task-specific qualifications and capacity are confirmed.

**Initial customer:** small property-management firms, owner-managed commercial buildings and local multi-site operators. They can hold several devices and face a recurring compliance workflow. Large institutions with formal procurement are a separate channel; homeowners are not the primary segment.

**Geography:** City of Ottawa only. Do not combine Gatineau: Quebec trade, tax, language and municipal rules require a separate decision.

### Choose one operating structure

#### Structure A — OAS fulfills the pilot (recommended)

- OAS is visibly the service business.
- Alex/OAS performs and invoices the represented work.
- OAS can use one genuine service-area GBP if all Google and operational facts support eligibility.
- OAS owns the domain, phone, customer relationship, analytics and service records.
- A later switch to referral/partner delivery requires a deliberate identity, consent, contract and GBP transition; the profile/reviews are not a portable rank-and-rent object.

This version maximizes learning and uses Alex's technical advantage. It also carries field-service insurance, warranty, WSIB and operational responsibility.

#### Structure B — one named provider fulfills from day one

- The page accurately states OAS's referral/marketing role and identifies the selected provider before information is shared, or the site accurately represents the exclusive provider under a controlled licence.
- The provider contracts, invoices and warrants the work.
- The provider owns its legitimate GBP and remains primary owner; OAS can be a permissioned manager.
- If the provider's GBP points at a website/number, the provider must have the ownership/control Google requires. An OAS-owned, provider-independent referral asset should remain profile-free.
- OAS approves a backup, but does not silently resell the same “exclusive” lead. Backup routing must match the customer's notice/consent.

This version is closer to the desired asset model but makes provider verification and consent the dominant work.

### Minimum website

Publish only after the identity and qualification gate passes:

- one backflow-testing service page;
- one property-manager page;
- a credentials/provider page;
- two original Ottawa guides: annual-test workflow and failed-test next steps;
- contact/quote, privacy and role-disclosure pages; and
- an honest Ottawa service-area page.

Add repair, survey or installation pages only for services actually available. Do not create Orléans, Kanata, Nepean and similar pages by replacing names. Use original field photos, expert-reviewed explanations, official Ottawa sources and clear last-reviewed dates.

### Demand validation before SEO scale

1. Build a 30-query English/French/problem/service inventory in Keyword Planner; use Trends only directionally.
2. Audit the commercial result page from Ottawa on mobile and desktop, recording ads, Maps, providers, directories, City results and actual search intent.
3. Complete ten property-manager/facility interviews about trigger, incumbent, device count, procurement, failure/repair and willingness to switch.
4. Obtain five comparable quotes and time/cost at least three legitimate jobs.
5. Census 20 potential providers; reach ten; obtain evidence from five; live-test at least two.
6. Run a capped exact/phrase paid-search experiment against the transparent landing page. Precommit the spend and qualification definition.
7. Produce downside/base/upside economics from completed and paid work, not clicks/calls.

### Measurement contract

One immutable `lead_id` should join:

`query/source → interaction → person/property → qualification → response → appointment → job → invoice → cleared payment/refund`

A qualified lead is a new, contactable, in-area prospect requesting a service the provider is legally and operationally able to deliver, with authority/access or a route to the responsible manager, and not spam, duplicate, an existing open customer need or an excluded service.

Record source and outcome separately. Search Console explains discovery; GA4 explains on-site behaviour; call/form tracking captures interaction; CRM/job/accounting records establish commercial truth. Call recording should remain off until there is an approved purpose, notice, consent, access and retention design.

## Commercial model if a partner receives the leads

### Stage 1 — capped learning trial

- 30 days or roughly ten accepted qualified leads.
- Written qualification, duplicate, existing-customer, territory and service definitions.
- 48-hour evidence-based invalidity window.
- Unique IDs and weekly disposition reconciliation.
- Volume and contractor spend cap.

### Stage 2 — repeatable fee

After at least 20 leads and 60 days, calculate:

`maximum rational PQL = average pre-HST collected invoice × completion rate × collection rate × contribution margin × acquisition-budget share`

If both parties remain positive under conservative inputs, use fixed monthly minimum plus PQL overage or a quarterly reset fixed fee based on the trailing median. Include a suspension/adjustment clause for material lead loss.

### Stage 3 — optional revenue share

Consider only after six months of reconciled lead-to-cleared-cash statements. Use attributable **collected revenue**, explicit refund/bad-debt treatment, a service-specific window and limited audit evidence. Do not use “net profit” without enumerated deductions. Do not take unrestricted QuickBooks/CRM access when an attributable-job view will do.

Equity, profit interests and acquisitions are different transactions requiring separate legal, tax, valuation, securities and governance work. They are outside this pilot.

## What Alex should learn personally

Alex should retain judgement over:

- niche economics, job costing and the downside case;
- backflow service/credential accuracy and customer-intent interviews;
- provider selection, trial-call/job review and complaint decisions;
- the qualified-lead and commercial-outcome definitions;
- reading Search Console/CRM/cash dashboards and diagnosing a drop;
- material content/safety claims and link-policy boundaries;
- model/partner/GBP identity decisions; and
- the 90/180/270-day continue, repair or kill decision.

These are the business. Outsourcing them before operating one cohort would leave OAS unable to tell growth from hidden risk.

## What to hire or delegate

Hire specialists for:

- Ontario commercial/privacy counsel: customer flow, privacy/terms, provider agreement, CASL/DNCL, liability and termination;
- CPA/bookkeeper: entity, fee characterization, HST, revenue recognition and chart of accounts;
- insurance broker: direct-provider versus marketer/referrer coverage, CGL/E&O/cyber and WSIB interaction;
- one technical SEO/security review before launch and after a major incident; and
- professional French review only if OAS commits to French service.

After the workflow is stable, a VA can perform synthetic tests, update dispositions from evidence, maintain credential expiries, prepare weekly exceptions and run approved content QA. Do not delegate root account ownership, provider approval, privacy incidents, complaint adjudication, legal claims or link-buying discretion.

## Success metrics and kill criteria

### Instrumentation and service gates

- At least 95% of inquiries attach to a CRM lead record.
- At least 90% of non-spam leads receive a qualification outcome within one business day.
- During stated business hours, target 90% of routed calls answered or called back within ten minutes; test whether that matters for scheduled backflow work.
- Paid jobs trace to lead, job, invoice and payment IDs.
- Invalid-lead disputes remain below 10% under a shared definition.
- Primary provider scores at least 80/100; backup passes all hard gates and one live test.
- No unresolved material complaint, privacy incident, identity problem or credential/insurance/WSIB lapse.

These are proposed OAS operating thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

### Commercial gates

- At 20 qualified leads or 60 days, each party can reproduce the same outcome ledger and fee.
- Observed first-job contribution remains positive after service delivery, acquisition/tracking and an explicit founder-time charge.
- The downside case does not rely on repair attachment, annual renewal, top-three rank, GBP traffic or future revenue share.
- Before asset two, the pilot has two consecutive cash-contribution-positive quarters and passed provider-exit and technical-restore drills.

### Immediate pause

- unsupported/fake identity or ineligible GBP;
- missing/expired provider qualification, insurance or required WSIB evidence;
- unsafe work, serious complaint or material misrepresentation;
- undisclosed lead sharing or call recording;
- suspected privacy breach;
- broken call/form route without a safe alternative; or
- manual action, hack or malware warning.

### Timed decisions

- **Day 30:** stop major build if ten customer interviews and three real job-cost observations cannot show an economically plausible, switchable market.
- **Day 90:** pause acquisition if the paid/relationship test cannot produce qualified demand at a cost compatible with conservative observed contribution, or if two viable providers cannot pass the gate.
- **Day 180:** do not expand content if relevant pages lack meaningful impressions/qualified inquiries after technical, demand and SERP causes are diagnosed.
- **Day 270:** kill or repurpose the asset if collected contribution, provider reliability or policy-compliant visibility still cannot meet the precommitted downside/payback case.

The exact loss cap and acceptable payback should be written before traffic begins. A reasonable planning discussion is whether base-case payback must be within 12 months and downside payback within 18; this is a risk preference, not an external fact.

## Twelve-month roadmap

### Months 1–2 — prove the market and choose the structure

- Decide whether Alex fulfills or an exclusive provider does.
- Complete City/task credential, insurance, WSIB and equipment checks.
- Conduct customer and provider interviews, SERP/query research and job costing.
- Choose one service, one customer segment and one geography.
- Obtain legal, accounting and broker reviews.
- Approve the data map, qualification rule and stage loss caps.

### Months 3–4 — launch the minimum system

- Publish the small expert-led site and official-source content.
- Configure Search Console, GA4, call/form tracking, CRM and invoice joins.
- Test consent, routing, duplicate/spam logic, failover, access and rollback.
- If eligible, launch one genuine operator GBP with evidence binder; otherwise remain profile-free or use the partner-owned profile.
- Start the capped paid/relationship demand test.

### Months 5–6 — operate manually and reconcile

- Alex reviews every qualified lead and material provider interaction.
- Measure answer, qualification, booking, completion, payment, complaint and contribution.
- Publish only improvements grounded in queries, interviews and field experience.
- Earn discovery through useful assets and genuine local relationships; no purchased-link package.
- Make the Day 180 continue/repair decision.

### Months 7–9 — stabilize the economics

- Reach the 20–30 qualified-lead evidence threshold where demand permits.
- Move a partner from capped PQL to fixed-plus-overage only if warranted.
- Test provider exit, technical restore, credential lapse and privacy incident.
- Document exception SOPs and delegate only stable checks.
- Make the Day 270 kill/repurpose/continue decision.

### Months 10–12 — earn the right to scale

- Complete two profitable-quarter evidence only if timing permits; otherwise continue observing.
- Audit account ownership, source data, content/link provenance, partner documents and complaints.
- Decide whether the asset remains an OAS service line, becomes a transparent partner asset or should be retired.
- Add no second niche until the first system is recoverable and cash-positive under fully loaded economics.

## The next decision Alex must make

**Will Alex/OAS personally fulfill the first 20–30 qualified backflow leads, or must the pilot be partner-operated from its first customer?**

The recommended answer is **personally fulfill first**, if Alex is willing and all current operational requirements are met. That gives OAS real first-party economics, customer-language, content, QA and service evidence. If the goal is immediately hands-off, select the exclusive-provider structure and accept its tradeoff: the provider—not OAS—must own/control the service identity and eligible GBP.

Do not purchase a domain, create a profile or draft the customer agreement until this choice is explicit.

## Confidence and unresolved facts

**High confidence:** the episode's ineligible/deceptive GBP tactics should be rejected; transparent identity/consent is required; one expert site is safer than cloned microsites; paid jobs and contribution are the right outcomes; provider quality is load-bearing; revenue share/equity are too early.

**Medium confidence:** backflow is the best OAS pilot, OAS-first fulfillment is the best learning structure, and the proposed gates/budget are conservative enough. These are reasoned recommendations based on Alex's background, not measured OAS results.

**Low confidence until fieldwork:** Ottawa search volume, addressable devices, prices, margin, incumbent lock-in, repair attachment, close rate, renewal rate, provider willingness and organic time-to-rank. No business plan should present those as facts until the pilot measures them.
