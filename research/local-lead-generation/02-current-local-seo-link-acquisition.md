# Report 2 — Current Local SEO and Legitimate Link Acquisition

**Decision context:** Organic local search for an OAS Ottawa backflow-service pilot  
**Research date:** 2026-07-15  
**Source priority:** Official Google Search documentation first; municipal and credentialing sources for Ottawa application. Episode claims are hypotheses only.

## Executive conclusion

OAS should build one transparent, expert-led Ottawa backflow service hub under its real brand—not a portfolio of near-duplicate geo-service microsites. Current Google guidance directly weakens two episode shortcuts: keywords in a domain name alone have “hardly any effect,” and there is no ideal word count. More text does not automatically outrank a shorter competitor.

The durable strategy is narrower and more demanding: publish distinct pages that solve real customer tasks; expose current credentials, service ownership and first-hand field expertise; make the site crawlable, fast and measurable; and earn discovery through genuine professional/community relationships and useful original resources. AI can help organize drafts, but every safety, regulatory and process claim needs expert review and direct sourcing. Mass-generated city/service variations create scaled-content and doorway risk.

Google’s July 10, 2026 guidance says its generative Search features still depend on core Search ranking and quality systems. There is no special schema, `llms.txt`, “chunking,” or separate AEO/GEO hack required. Pages must be indexed and eligible for Search, while non-commodity, expert content and a clear technical structure remain the recommended foundation.

Treat the episode’s 2016 outcomes as historical anecdotes. Google’s official status history lists repeated core and spam updates through June 2026, and current policy explicitly addresses AI-scaled content, doorway sites, exact-match domains and link manipulation. A new site should be funded as an experiment with no ranking guarantee.

## Episode hypothesis

The episode suggests that an entire site about one local service can beat a competitor’s short inner page; that separate service pages, exact-match geo domains, large amounts of content, FAQs and backlinks can produce rankings; and that AI makes the model easier to scale.

Testable elements remain:

- A page dedicated to a distinct service intent can be more useful than a generic bullet list.
- Clear phone/form conversion and mobile usability matter commercially.
- Competitor research can reveal unanswered customer questions.

The unsupported leaps are that 1,500 words beat 400, an exact-match domain supplies a meaningful shortcut, and many AI-assisted service/location pages constitute quality. Google’s current documentation says otherwise.

## Current evidence

### 1. Organic eligibility is foundational, not guaranteed

Google describes Search as crawling, indexing and serving. Even a compliant page is not guaranteed to be crawled, indexed or shown, and changes can take hours to several months.

For OAS, the minimum technical foundation is:

- HTTPS, stable hosting and no blocked essential CSS/JavaScript
- crawlable navigation and ordinary `<a href>` internal links
- one canonical URL per substantive page
- XML sitemap submitted in Search Console
- unique, accurate titles and meta descriptions
- mobile-equivalent content and conversion controls
- visible text for important service and compliance information
- truthful structured data matching the page
- security, backups and uptime monitoring

Google’s Core Web Vitals targets are LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds and CLS below 0.1; they do not guarantee ranking.

### 2. Helpful, expert content beats commodity volume

Google says its systems prioritize helpful, reliable, people-first content. Its self-assessment asks whether a page contains original information, research or analysis; adds value beyond rewritten sources; and demonstrates experience, expertise, authority and trust. The current generative-AI guide recommends unique viewpoints and non-commodity content rather than facts any model could restate.

Applied to backflow, “Ottawa backflow testing” copy assembled from competitor pages is commodity content. Defensible OAS content would include:

- an expert-reviewed explanation of the City’s annual-test and five-year-survey workflows
- an original booking checklist: CCN, device details, access, shutdown constraints and prior reports
- annotated, permissioned field photos showing common device/clearance/reporting issues
- a clear distinction between test, survey, repair, replacement and permit work
- what happens after a failed test, including who may repair and how retesting/reporting works
- first-hand guidance for property managers coordinating multiple sites or devices
- author/reviewer identity, credentials, source links and a last-reviewed date

Each page should be only as long as required to complete the user’s task.

### 3. Build a service architecture, not doorways

A reasonable starting architecture is:

- `/backflow-testing/`
- `/backflow-repair/`
- `/premise-isolation-survey/`
- `/backflow-installation-permits/` (only if OAS is qualified and actually offers it)
- `/property-managers/`
- `/guides/ottawa-annual-backflow-test/`
- `/guides/failed-backflow-test-next-steps/`
- `/about/credentials/`
- one honest service-area/contact page

Each URL needs distinct intent. Do not clone pages for Orleans, Nepean, Kanata and every suburb by swapping place names. Google’s doorway policy covers regional pages that funnel users to one destination and substantially similar pages for similar queries. Its scaled-content policy applies regardless of whether humans or AI produced the pages.

Require location-specific proof before adding a location page: real availability, unique logistics, permissioned examples or a different municipal process.

### 4. Exact-match domains and raw word count are poor foundations

Google’s Starter Guide says keywords in a domain or URL path alone have hardly any ranking effect. Its ranking-systems guide says the exact-match-domain system prevents domains designed to match queries from receiving too much credit. The Starter Guide also says content length alone does not matter for ranking.

For OAS, this favours a trustworthy brand architecture over `ottawabackflowtesting...` microsites. A descriptive page title and URL are still useful to humans, but the business case should not depend on the domain keyword.

### 5. AI Search does not require a separate playbook

Google’s December 2025 AI-features guide says AI Overviews and AI Mode use indexed Search content and need no additional technical requirements or special optimization. The newer July 10, 2026 guide goes further: foundational SEO remains relevant; valuable, expert, non-commodity content is preferred; special AI markup and `llms.txt` are unnecessary; “chunking” and rewriting for AI are not required; and inauthentic mentions are not a durable strategy.

**Inference — high confidence.** Commercial searches may still lead directly to providers while AI surfaces answer informational questions. Give the answer, then offer a relevant action.

**Unknown.** Google does not disclose query-level inclusion criteria or click-through rates for OAS’s market. The older guide says AI-feature traffic is included in the normal Web performance report; the July 2026 guide describes a Generative AI performance report. The likely explanation is a newer reporting capability, but availability and historical coverage must be confirmed inside OAS’s Search Console account.

### 6. Legitimate links come from real value and relationships

Google says it discovers most new pages through links and recommends promotion through social media, community engagement, advertising, word of mouth and offline promotion. This is not permission to manufacture PageRank.

Its link-spam policy prohibits, among other things:

- buying or selling links for ranking purposes
- exchanging goods/services for ranking links
- excessive reciprocal linking
- automated link creation
- contractually requiring an unqualified link
- paid articles or press releases that pass ranking credit
- low-quality directory links, template/footer links and comment spam

Paid placements are permissible as advertising when marked `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow` so they do not pass ranking credit.

For the OAS pilot, pursue links/referrals in this order:

1. **Credential and compliance discovery:** maintain accurate OWWA tester-directory and Skilled Trades Ontario records. These are first trust assets even if they do not provide a followed website link.
2. **Real organizational profiles:** join an Ottawa business, facilities or property-management organization only if the membership has business value. Complete the member profile honestly; do not join dozens of directories for links.
3. **Original utility asset:** publish an Ottawa annual-testing checklist or property-manager device inventory template, reviewed against the current City by-law. Promote it personally to facilities professionals and plumbing partners because it saves them work.
4. **Expert education:** offer a factual webinar, toolbox talk or article to a real local association. The host controls whether and how it links; do not require keyword-rich anchor text.
5. **Supplier and partner relationships:** seek editorial inclusion only where OAS has a genuine commercial or technical relationship and the page helps the supplier’s/customer’s audience.
6. **Permissioned local proof:** publish a case note showing process, constraints and outcome without invented savings or confidential building details. A customer may choose to reference it.
7. **Community sponsorship/advertising:** evaluate it for audience reach; require `sponsored`/`nofollow` when paid.

Test every prospect: “Would this still be worthwhile if Google ignored the link?”

## Ottawa-specific application

Identify OAS as the service business and state who performs each service. Verify credentials and link to authoritative directories. Cite the City, and use original field knowledge—access, device information, reporting, failures and property-manager coordination—as the content advantage.

Avoid a generic “we serve every city” footprint. The initial scope is the City of Ottawa. Gatineau needs a separate legal, licensing, language and search analysis before any page is published. English and French Ottawa content should be professionally equivalent, connected with correct `hreflang` only if OAS can serve both languages; automated thin translation is not enough.

`LocalBusiness` structured data may be appropriate for a real eligible OAS service business. It must match visible name, contact, area and hours, and it does not guarantee a rich result. Do not mark up fabricated reviews, service locations, prices or credentials.

## Practical pilot exercise

### Phase A — build the evidence-led minimum site

1. Verify the real business identity, service ownership, credentials, insurance and City/BSI workflow.
2. Produce an intent map from Keyword Planner, customer interviews and live SERPs. Approve no page whose purpose differs only by a place-name swap.
3. Publish the core testing page, property-manager page, credentials page, contact/privacy pages, and two original Ottawa guides.
4. Add first-hand photos/diagrams with permission and meaningful alt text. Link directly to the current City and credential sources.
5. Implement titles, descriptions, canonicals, sitemap, robots controls, internal links and truthful structured data.
6. Verify the site in Search Console; inspect every launch URL; record index state and any structured-data issues.

### Phase B — acquire and measure

7. Create one useful downloadable/checklist asset without gating its core information.
8. Contact 20 relevant local organizations, suppliers, property managers and trade relationships individually. Ask for feedback/use, not “a dofollow link.” Log responses and any editorial links/referrals.
9. Measure weekly for 180 days: indexed pages; impressions/clicks by query/page/device; non-brand versus brand; calls/forms; qualified/booked/completed/collected jobs; referring-domain traffic; Core Web Vitals; and content updates.
10. Review at days 30, 90 and 180. Improve pages from real query and customer evidence. Do not add dozens of pages because ranking is slow.

## Completion test

The SEO capability is ready for a pilot only when:

- every indexable URL has a distinct user task and accountable expert reviewer;
- no page depends on copied, spun or location-swapped content;
- Google can crawl/render the pages and the sitemap/canonicals are correct;
- all important launch URLs are inspected and index status recorded;
- mobile call/form conversion works and carries source attribution into the job log;
- structured data matches visible facts and passes validation;
- Core Web Vitals are measured, with remediation owners for failures;
- there is a 20-prospect legitimate promotion list and no paid/exchanged/automated link scheme;
- success is defined as qualified and collected work, not rank or raw traffic;
- the 180-day budget and stop criteria assume Google may never rank the site prominently.

## Unknowns and conflicts

- Google does not publish ranking weights, a link quantity target or a guaranteed time to rank.
- The current Ottawa SERP and competitor backlink profiles require a dated live audit; official documentation cannot supply them.
- Search Console’s new Generative AI report may not yet have data/availability for this property; verify in-product.
- It is unknown whether informational AI surfaces will increase, reduce or merely change clicks for Ottawa backflow queries.
- The episode’s “longer page wins” conflicts with Google’s explicit no-ideal-word-count guidance.
- The episode’s exact-match-domain emphasis conflicts with Google’s statement that domain keywords alone have hardly any effect and with its exact-match-domain adjustment system.
- The episode’s proposed multi-site/location scaling can cross into doorway or scaled-content abuse if pages are substantially similar or exist mainly to funnel leads.
- Backlinks can correlate with visibility, but the incremental causal value of any one legitimate link cannot be promised.

## Why 2016 outcomes are not a baseline

The episode’s first sites date to 2016. Since then, Google has integrated anti-link-spam systems, made helpful-content signals part of core ranking, adopted mobile-first indexing, added Core Web Vitals, formalized scaled-content and doorway policies, deployed AI Overviews/AI Mode, and continued broad core and spam updates. The official ranking dashboard records multiple core/spam updates in 2024, 2025 and 2026, including March and May 2026 core updates and a June 2026 spam update.

Older sites also have links, query history, brand searches and operating data a new domain lacks. “Ranked in two or three months” is not a forecast; measure OAS’s own impressions, qualified leads and collected contribution.

## Failure modes

- Buying an exact-match domain and mistaking it for strategy
- Padding every service page to an arbitrary word count
- Creating suburb pages with only names changed
- Publishing AI drafts without field and regulatory review
- Copying competitor FAQs, reviews or City language without original value
- Buying guest posts, directory packages or automated links
- Requiring partners/customers to provide followed links
- Using false business identity, credentials, locations or reviews
- Treating structured data or `llms.txt` as a ranking lever
- Reporting rank, impressions or calls without job quality and collected revenue
- Launching many sites before one site demonstrates policy-compliant economics
- Ignoring security, plugin/framework updates, forms, accessibility and mobile conversion
