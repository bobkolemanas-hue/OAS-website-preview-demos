# Report 5 — Contractor Recruitment and Quality Control

**Decision context:** Ottawa/Ontario local lead-generation business  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-07-15  
**Evidence convention:** **Fact** = supported by a cited authoritative source. **Vendor benchmark** = measured by a vendor on its own customer/sample base and useful only directionally. **Inference** = recommended operating judgment. **Unknown** = must be measured or confirmed before launch.

## Executive conclusion

The limiting resource is not a website; it is a contractor who is simultaneously eligible, responsive, competent at estimating, able to absorb incremental work, honest about outcomes, and safe to place in front of OAS-generated customers. The episode itself acknowledges that finding the right buyer/operator is harder than generating leads. Treat that as a hypothesis worth testing, not proof that most contractors will accept or convert leads.

For OAS, the best first partner market is backflow testing and related commercial plumbing because Alex can independently judge technical quality and already understands the buyer and regulatory context. Even here, a name in a directory is only an eligibility signal. Ottawa requires qualified people for different backflow functions, annual device testing, current tester credentials, registered reporting, and calibrated equipment [S1–S4]. OAS should recruit a **primary plus an approved overflow provider**, not award exclusivity to the first interested contractor.

The launch gate should be: at least two providers pass documentary verification; each completes a small, instrumented lead trial; one meets service and reporting thresholds without a material complaint; and the backup can accept overflow. If OAS cannot establish this before building significant SEO inventory, the business is supply-constrained and should pause.

## Episode hypothesis to test

The captions support these episode claims, but none is independently verified:

- Contractors already buying advertising are more likely to understand the value of leads.
- A few free leads can test follow-up behaviour before a commercial arrangement.
- The operator should observe answer quality, response speed, communication, and whether the contractor reports closed work honestly.
- A poor recipient can waste valuable leads; the right recipient makes the asset relatively low-touch.
- The guest says most arrangements are flat fee and that finding a serious buyer is harder than producing leads.

These are sensible screening hypotheses. They do **not** establish Ottawa partner supply, acceptance rates, close rates, workmanship quality, or willingness to share CRM/invoice data.

## Current evidence and operating implications

### 1. Partner-market liquidity is unproven

**Fact:** OWWA maintains a current directory of certified Cross Connection Control testers in good standing and says certification lasts up to five years [S2, S3]. Ottawa directs affected owners to qualified testers and uses BSI to manage device compliance and annual testing [S1, S4]. These sources provide a prospecting pool.

**Unknown:** The accessible sources do not establish how many listed people actively serve Ottawa, have spare capacity, perform only tests versus surveys/repairs, or want purchased leads. A credential directory is not a marketplace-liquidity study.

**Ottawa recruitment test:** Build a 20-candidate census from OWWA, BSI/City-qualified listings, local commercial-plumbing firms, and trusted trade referrals. Record: decision-maker reached, service functions, territory, response time, weekly capacity, minimum job economics, willingness to report outcomes, and commercial interest. Do not infer liquidity until at least five pass the document screen and two pass a live-lead trial.

### 2. Documentary eligibility is a hard gate, not a score

For every provider, verify and save dated evidence:

1. **Legal identity:** Ontario Business Registry search, exact legal/operating names, address, HST number if applicable, signing authority [S5].
2. **Trade status:** Skilled Trades Ontario Public Register for each person doing compulsory-trade work [S6].
3. **Backflow testing:** current OWWA certificate number and expiry in the OWWA directory [S2, S3].
4. **Ottawa functions:** evidence that the person/company is accepted for the function being sold—test, survey, install, repair, relocate—and can submit through the City/BSI process. Do not treat “certified tester” as authority for every function [S1–S4].
5. **Test equipment:** current calibration documentation. Ottawa’s program requirements call for annual calibration data [S4].
6. **Installation/repair authority:** OWWA states installation and repair must be performed by a journeyman plumber or registered apprentice under journeyman supervision; Ottawa separately describes qualified persons and licensed-contractor employment [S1, S2].
7. **WSIB:** current clearance, or documented independent-operator ruling where legitimately applicable. A clearance confirms registration and good standing; it is not proof of workmanship [S7].
8. **Insurance:** current commercial general liability certificate sent by the broker/insurer, named legal entity matching the contract, relevant operations included, and expiry diary. **Unknown:** the reviewed Ottawa backflow sources do not state a universal insurance minimum for this private arrangement. OAS should have an Ontario broker and counsel set limits rather than inventing a regulatory threshold.
9. **Regulatory history:** Ontario Consumer Beware List plus recent charges/compliance notices [S8]. Its published window is limited; a clean result is not a quality certificate.

Any mismatch in legal name, expired credential, refusal to provide a broker-issued certificate, or inability to identify who will actually perform the work is a stop condition.

### 3. Responsiveness and call handling must be measured locally

Two vendor studies demonstrate both the importance of measurement and the danger of importing a benchmark:

- **Vendor benchmark — CallRail (2025):** analysis of 1.1 million de-identified conversations reported a 14% missed-call rate for home services [S9].
- **Vendor benchmark — Invoca (2026):** analysis of 70 million calls across its customers reported that only 52% of home-services callers reached a person; 38% of answered calls were classified as leads and 45% of those converted on the call [S10].

The apparent conflict likely reflects different customers, definitions, filters, and channels. Neither study is Ottawa-specific or backflow-specific. OAS should not promise economics using either.

**Pilot service targets (OAS policy, not industry facts):** during stated business hours, 90% of routed calls answered live or called back within 10 minutes; 95% of leads dispositioned in the CRM by end of next business day; no lead left untouched for more than one business day. Backflow compliance work is often scheduled rather than emergency work, so OAS should measure a separate email/form response target (same business day) instead of blindly applying emergency-plumbing speed.

### 4. Estimating and sales competence are distinct from technical competence

A technically qualified tester may still mishandle intake, fail to ask for the appointment, quote vaguely, or ignore a property manager’s documentation needs. The 2026 Invoca vendor study reports only 45% of home-services calls included an ask for the sale/appointment, despite much stronger greeting and closing scores [S10]. This is directional evidence, not an Ottawa baseline.

Score five observed behaviours on every trial call:

- professional identification and explanation of the next step;
- capture of property, device, CCN/notice, access, due date, and decision-maker data;
- correct separation of test, survey, repair, replacement, and permit needs;
- a definite appointment/inspection request or documented reason it cannot be booked;
- written estimate with scope, exclusions, HST, reporting/administration charges, timing, and who owns follow-up.

OAS should listen only where lawful and disclosed. If calls are recorded, PIPEDA guidance says the caller must be told, the purpose stated, and consent obtained; retention and access also need controls [S11]. A “secret survey” used to obtain pricing or close data under another pretext is not an acceptable QA method.

### 5. Reputation and workmanship accountability require a closed loop

Public ratings are useful for discovery but vulnerable to selection bias and do not prove current insurance, licensing, or commercial competence. Use three recent references with similar work, then independently contact them. For backflow, ask whether the provider arrived as scheduled, submitted correct records promptly, explained failed devices, obtained approval before repair, and resolved deficiencies.

The provider—not OAS—should contract for and warrant the field work. However, OAS cannot ignore complaints because its brand and routing decision caused the introduction. The partner agreement should require: licence/insurance maintenance; legal and code compliance; written estimates; no unauthorized work; complaint notice within one business day; a documented correction plan; cooperation with chargebacks/investigations; and indemnity reviewed by Ontario counsel. OAS needs its own escalation log and the ability to suspend routing immediately.

## Recruitment scorecard

First apply every hard gate above. Then score qualified candidates out of 100:

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Capacity and responsiveness | 20 | stated slots verified against trial answer/callback and scheduling data |
| Intake, estimating and sales | 20 | scored calls, written estimate, appointment conversion |
| Technical/compliance execution | 20 | correct function/credentials, calibration, sample reports, deficiency handling |
| Customer experience and accountability | 15 | references, complaint handling, punctuality, rework evidence |
| Data/reporting integrity | 15 | CRM dispositions, invoice reconciliation, privacy/security cooperation |
| Commercial fit | 10 | service area, job economics, payment reliability, willingness to use the pilot rules |

Pass at 80, with no dimension below 60% of its available points. A 95-point sales operator with an expired credential still fails because gates are not compensable.

## OAS backflow-specific provider design

Use function-specific lanes:

- **Testing lane:** current OWWA certification, accepted municipal/BSI registration, calibrated kit, accurate same-day/next-day submission, ability to handle failed-test communication.
- **Survey lane:** proof the individual is qualified for Ottawa premise-isolation surveys and can produce a complete five-year survey package.
- **Repair/install lane:** journeyman/master-plumber authority, licensed-contractor relationship where required, permits/inspection competence, parts access, and capacity for urgent deficiencies.
- **Portfolio lane:** commercial insurance, property-management references, purchase-order/invoice discipline, site access protocols, bilingual service where required, and ability to manage multiple devices/locations.

Alex’s trade background is an advantage: he can review sample test reports, estimates, and failure explanations. Preserve that technical QA rather than delegating partner approval to an SEO or sales assistant.

## Skills and tools required

- Partner sourcing database and outreach scripts compliant with Canadian telemarketing/CASL rules.
- Credential-expiry register with reminders for OWWA, trade status, WSIB, insurance, calibration, and City/BSI eligibility.
- CRM with unique lead IDs, source, timestamps, consent record, routing, disposition, estimate, completion, payment, complaint, and rework fields.
- Call routing with business-hours/overflow rules; recording off by default until notice, consent, retention, and access are configured.
- Standard call and estimate QA rubric; reference-check script; monthly partner scorecard.
- Privacy-safe evidence exchange: do not demand unrestricted QuickBooks or full-CRM access merely to prove a small pilot.
- Incident and replacement runbook, including consumer notification when a lead must be transferred.

## Overflow and replacement plan

Approve one primary and one backup before paid acquisition. Route by service/function and postal code, not merely round-robin. The primary declares daily capacity; routing pauses when full. A missed call rolls to the backup only if the consumer was told that OAS may connect them with a qualified independent provider. Never silently send the same “exclusive” lead to multiple providers.

Immediate suspension triggers: credential/insurance lapse; material misrepresentation; unsafe or unauthorized work; refusal to report outcomes; privacy incident; two substantiated complaints; or repeated SLA failure. Replacement should preserve the consumer’s choice, not automatically transfer an active job. The original contractor retains obligations for work already contracted; OAS reroutes only new/unaccepted leads unless the customer requests otherwise.

## Practical pilot exercise

1. Build the 20-candidate Ottawa census and complete 10 owner conversations.
2. Obtain full evidence packs from five; independently verify every credential.
3. Run scripted test calls during three time windows. Do not use false identities to collect personal or transaction data.
4. Select three candidates for a capped trial of 3–5 qualified leads each. Use the same lead definition and comparable time blocks.
5. Reconcile every lead to contact, appointment, estimate, completed work, collected revenue, complaint, and disposition.
6. Select a primary and backup only after a review call and signed operating agreement.

### Completion test

This research gap is closed only when: two Ottawa providers pass all hard gates; at least 10 real qualified leads have been routed; at least 90% have complete dispositions; response, booking, completion, and complaint data are observable; one provider scores at least 80/100; the backup has accepted at least one test lead; and OAS can replace a provider without losing the domain, tracking channel, consent record, or unresolved customer history.

## Unknowns and conflicts to resolve

- Number of active Ottawa providers with real incremental capacity and commercial interest.
- Current City/BSI registration steps, filing fees, authorized-function distinctions, and whether any requirements changed under Water By-law 2025-227. Confirm directly with the City before launch.
- Local test, survey, repair, and replacement prices; contribution margins; seasonality; and multi-device portfolio economics.
- Appropriate CGL/E&O limits and whether OAS’s role is insured as marketing, referral, or contractor management.
- Whether French intake is necessary for the selected Ottawa/Gatineau territory; Quebec work is a separate legal/regulatory market.
- Vendor benchmark conflict: 14% missed calls (CallRail) versus 52% reaching a person (Invoca). Only OAS pilot data can settle the relevant baseline.

## Failure modes

- Building/ranking inventory before proving two viable partners.
- Treating a directory listing or star rating as quality assurance.
- Allowing one certificate holder to cover unqualified field staff or functions outside the certificate.
- Sending leads to a contractor who is technically good but cannot answer, estimate, or report.
- Paying for “closed” jobs without invoice/payment reconciliation—or demanding excessive customer data to obtain it.
- Granting exclusivity without capacity and performance floors.
- Recording calls or transferring lead data without meaningful notice and consent.
- Letting a partner lapse in insurance, WSIB, calibration, or certification between annual reviews.
- OAS appearing to be the field contractor while disclaiming all responsibility after a complaint.
